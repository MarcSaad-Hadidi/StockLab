# Test payoff weighting for boosted sell decisions

The retained 28-feature model earns 18.63% per year with 24.77% maximum drawdown in the full account. Removing its 200-session sell confirmation lowers drawdown to 20.12% but drops growth to 15.32%. A 20-session direction label gives the same importance to a small move near the cost hurdle and a large loss. This test changes the learning objective for sales while keeping every entry intent from the retained model. No return for this recipe was calculated before this plan.

1. Use the immutable original-five 16,345-row feature frame and existing binary label `open[t+21]/open[t+1]-1 > .002`. Reconstruct returns and known dates from the frozen stock opens as a check. Use the exact nine original annual training populations, ordered 28 inputs, four-year window and strict `target_date_20 < first_prediction` purge. Keep HGBT hyperparameters, deterministic seed and one OpenMP thread. There are no new features, symbols, labels or calibrations.
2. Train one sell model per year. For each training row compute `m = abs(forward_return_20 - .002)`. Clip `m` at the 90th percentile of that year's eligible training magnitudes using linear quantile interpolation. Divide each clipped magnitude by the mean clipped magnitude for its own feature date. Thus every complete five-symbol date has total weight five. If all five magnitudes on one date are zero, assign equal weight one. The percentile is determined from training only. Exclude unknown labels from fitting and scoring metrics. Retain them for prediction.
3. Export the model output as `payoff_weighted_up_score`. It is a score from a weighted objective and is not a calibrated probability of a price increase. Keep cached raw BUY decisions from the original 28-feature model, with every BUY date, symbol, amount and probability unchanged. On other rows a SELL requires weighted score ≤ .35, `close_ma200 < 0` and `momentum_20 < 0`. A retained BUY takes precedence over a new SELL for the same key. Export these collisions and both scores on all 11,140 evaluation rows. The legacy initial-capital sizing, 20% per-symbol purchase cap, 10% equity per purchase, opening execution order, 176 verified gross dividends and cost accounting stay fixed.
4. Evaluate exactly three new accounts from new cash of USD 100,000: 2018–2026 through November 11 at 5 bps fee plus 5 bps slippage and 25+25 bps, and a fresh 2021–2026 account at 5+5 bps. No deposits, leverage, short exposure, terminal liquidation, new provider calls or same-score baseline replays. Copy SPY, retained immediate and retained BUY-only references with their file bindings. Record all orders, fills, yearly performance, cost basis, cash and dividend accounting. Compute paired differences against both controls for each account.
5. Compare weighted Brier loss to the forecast-year causal weighted training rate and to the original HGBT score using the same matured rows and evaluation weights. Weight evaluation rows by their realized magnitude with the already fixed annual cap and equal mass per observed date. This is a retrospective weighted loss diagnostic. It does not alter any decision or prove calibration. Report it for the full and fresh-2021 ranges alongside ordinary Brier loss.
6. Qualification requires full and fresh base-cost CAGR to exceed 20% and corresponding SPY, their drawdowns to be below SPY and the matching retained model, an executed model sale in each, and terminal wealth to exceed corresponding BUY-only controls. The high-cost full account must independently exceed 20% and both same-cost control CAGRs with lower drawdown than the same-cost retained model and a model sale. The weighted Brier loss must improve over both comparators in both intervals. Verify the exact gate values from frozen reports. If a gate fails, preserve the result and keep the retained reference. Even a pass on these repeatedly inspected five-survivor histories is exploratory and requires genuinely prospective evidence before activation.
